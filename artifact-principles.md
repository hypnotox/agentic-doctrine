# Artifact principles

These principles specialize the [general principles](general-principles.md) for creating and maintaining documents, game assets, and other lasting outputs. Apply [visual design](visual-design-principles.md), [typography](typography-principles.md), and [animation](animation-principles.md) guidance where those concerns matter.

## 1. Let purpose determine form.

Shape the artifact around its intended use and the people or systems that will use it. Let that purpose determine its form, organization, and level of detail.

Give its contents a reason to be there, including expressive or narrative purposes. For fictional text, consider its author, intended reader, setting, and status. Choose wording and voice that fit that context; repeated technical language or instructions do not create credibility by themselves.

## 2. Preserve meaning and necessary context.

Preserve the context needed to interpret, use, and maintain the artifact correctly, including relevant reasoning, design intent, and usage constraints. For documents, prefer useful synthesis to exhaustive reproduction.

## 3. Make the status of the content clear.

Distinguish proposals, decisions, and observations where the difference matters. Keep intended behavior, current implementation, verification, and acceptance distinguishable. Make unresolved or uncertain content apparent.

## 4. Let structure serve the content.

Choose organization and presentation that help its users find and use what matters. Adapt templates to the content rather than creating content to fill them.

## 5. Keep artifacts useful over time.

Maintain information and assets that are meant to stay current. Preserve history when it remains useful, without leaving superseded material looking current.
