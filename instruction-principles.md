# Instruction principles

These principles specialize the [general principles](general-principles.md) for writing and maintaining guidance.

## 1. State the intended behavior directly.

Describe what should happen in concrete terms. Include explanation when it helps the reader understand or apply the instruction.

## 2. Make applicability and obligation clear.

Make clear when guidance applies and whether it expresses a requirement, a default, or an option. State conditions that materially affect its use.

## 3. Preserve room for judgment.

Specify the outcomes and boundaries that matter. Prescribe a method or exact value when following it matters to the result; otherwise leave routine choices open. Do not turn the current implementation into a mandatory procedure without a reason grounded in the intended outcome.

## 4. Distinguish instructions from supporting material.

Make clear which content directs behavior and which provides context, explanation, or examples. Examples should illustrate guidance without silently adding requirements. Where an example could be mistaken for a requirement, clarify which details matter and which are incidental.

## 5. Keep the guidance coherent.

Treat instructions as a coherent whole. Resolve contradictions, consolidate repeated guidance, and remove instructions that no longer serve a purpose.
