# Planning principles

These principles specialize the [working principles](working-principles.md) for implementation plans. Apply them when settled work benefits from an explicit route; keep planning proportionate to the task.

## 1. Plan from a settled direction.

Sequence the agreed outcome and its established constraints. Surface unresolved material choices or blockers rather than silently deciding them inside plan steps.

## 2. Keep every step tied to the outcome.

Include only work required by the outcome, established constraints, or evidenced risks, including necessary enabling changes. Exclude speculative flexibility, hardening, compatibility work, and unrelated cleanup.

## 3. Plan semantic changes, not edit scripts.

Divide work around meaningful changes in behavior, responsibilities, state, or integration. Explain what changes and why without narrating individual edits.

## 4. Make specificity earn its place.

Use enough detail to preserve settled decisions and prevent consequential ambiguity. Prefer intended changes, boundaries, and relationships over exact edits. Include files, symbols, commands, data shapes, or local mechanics only when they are themselves important to the plan; their being known is not enough.

## 5. Expose real dependencies and integration points.

Order work according to actual dependencies. Identify where separately changed parts meet and keep shared or integration concerns in one place. Do not manufacture phases or coordination for work that is naturally linear.

## 6. Keep the route revisable.

Treat the plan as the best current route, not a frozen contract. Evidence may change files, ordering, or mechanics while the outcome and established constraints remain authoritative. Surface changes that alter either.
